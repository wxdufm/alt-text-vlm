Alt text guidelines
Basics
The purpose of the alt text is to provide an equivalent textual alternative for the album artwork to users who cannot see it, in accordance with WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.1.1 (Non-text Content).
The alt text should be concise. As a project guideline, descriptions should generally not exceed 25 words or 3 sentences, unless additional detail is necessary to preserve important visual information. (Note: WCAG does not prescribe a maximum length; this is a project-specific guideline.)
The first sentence should describe the primary visual subject or composition of the cover.
Additional sentences may describe typography, color palette, artistic style, or other visually significant elements.
The description should be understandable without requiring the listener to see the image.
The purpose of the description is not to enumerate every visible detail, but to provide a blind or low-vision user with an understanding of the artwork that is functionally equivalent to what a sighted user would reasonably perceive when viewing the cover in the context of a musical release. The level of detail should be sufficient to communicate the artwork's primary visual content, composition, and aesthetic character without becoming exhaustive. 

Context
The image being described is an album cover. The description should therefore be written with the assumption that the artwork is part of a musical release rather than a standalone photograph or illustration.
Potential use cases include:
Music streaming and playback interfaces
Album review websites
Digital music libraries
Discogs and MusicBrainz integrations
Radio station playlists
Screen reader users browsing music collections
Music archive and preservation projects

What to include
Describe information that contributes to understanding the artwork.
Examples include:
Main subject(s)
Composition and layout
Artistic medium (photograph, painting, collage, illustration, digital art, etc.)
Dominant colors and color palette
Typography and visible text when it is readable and visually significant
Visual style (minimalist, psychedelic, collage, zine-inspired, geometric, surreal, etc.)
Overall aesthetic impression when it is directly supported by visible visual features
Relationships between major visual elements

Do not assume that alternate pressings or releases share identical artwork unless confirmed by image matching.
